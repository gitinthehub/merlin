# "The Last Signal of Oronath" — the arc spec

The owner's design for the first real Oronath arc, written down as the content contract for roadmap item **2.8**. The engine core (2.1–2.3), the playable loop (2.4–2.5) and the arc-builder pipeline (2.6–2.7) already exist; this file says what the arc must contain, and the engine's validator is the gate that decides whether the built artefact is acceptable.

## Premise

A cinematic, post-catastrophe wilderness story in four chapters. Three travellers reach a dead tree carrying a signal that should not still be running. What they find there is older than the village they came from and is, in one way or another, about each of them.

Tone: vivid, specific, visual. Clear cause → effect → consequence. No vague resolutions.

## Cast and the threads they must close

| Character | Traits (already in `characters.js`) | Thread | What closing it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **John** | Stealth, Night vision, Endurance | `john-memory` (memory/identity) | John learns the truth about his own people — that the guardians are what his people became, or were made into. |
| **Joel** | Strength, An axe with unknown carvings | `joel-legacy` (legacy) | Joel learns what the carvings mean: an ancestral map, and an inheritance he can choose to carry or refuse. |
| **Rafe** | Speed, Sniper, Alien origin | `rafe-return` (return-home) | Rafe chooses between the world he came from and the one he is standing in. |

All three threads must be resolved by every ending. The validator enforces this; do not add an ending that leaves one open.

## Chapters

The owner specified four. The validator accepts 3–5; keep four.

### Chapter 1 — The Signal Tree

**Setting:** an eighty-foot dead tree standing alone on a plain of ash, its veins glowing blue under the bark. A crystal pulses near the top, in time with something that is not a heartbeat.

**Objective:** get the crystal down intact.

**Choices (the owner's four, in the owner's words):**

1. **Joel climbs** — 2d6, the owner's threshold is 9+.
2. **John stealth-climbs** — d20, the owner's threshold is 11+.
3. **Rafe shoots it down** — d10, the owner's threshold is 8+.
4. **The team combo** — John scouts the route (d20, 11+), which makes Joel's climb easier; Rafe covers them (d10, 8+).

**Difficulty mapping:** the engine's tiers are d20-based (easy 8, medium 11, hard 14, very hard 16) and `characters.js` traits shift the DC. Map the owner's numbers onto the engine's own tiers and DCs, keep each choice's stated feel (Joel's climb is the teamwork/force check, Rafe's shot is the precision check, John's is the skill check), and **write the mapping down** in the build notes. Do not invent a second difficulty system.

**Consequences:** taking the crystal down cleanly, taking it down loudly, and bringing the tree down instead are all different roads. A complication here is not "you failed to get it" — it is something that costs them later.

### Chapter 2 — The Echo Field

**Setting:** a flat white waste where sound arrives before it is made. The field builds illusions out of what each traveller remembers, and it is very good at John.

**Objective:** cross it, and keep hold of the difference between what happened and what the field wants them to think happened.

**Requirements:** John's past is triggered here — this is the chapter where `john-memory` starts to move rather than being touched at the end. Truth-versus-illusion must be a real check the player can fail, with the failure consequence being a *wrong but consistent* memory that colours what the later chapters mean, not "you take damage".

### Chapter 3 — The Axe Lock

**Setting:** a vault door under the ash, its lock cut in the same shapes as Joel's carvings. The door is not locked against a key; it is locked against being understood.

**Objective:** decode the carvings and align them.

**Requirements:** **failure triggers guardians** — this is the owner's explicit consequence and it must be a distinct branch, not a retry. This chapter is where `joel-legacy` moves. The carvings must mean something specific (an ancestral map) that the player can learn here or fail to learn.

### Chapter 4 — The Signal Core

**Setting:** what the vault was holding: an alien device still running, tied to Rafe's origin, and to the signal the tree has been repeating.

**Objective:** decide what happens to it.

**The final choice must be the owner's three:** **destroy it**, **activate it**, or **use it as a portal** — with those three leading to genuinely different endings, not three flavours of the same ending.

## Endings

At least one ending per final choice, each resolving all three threads. The owner's pre-written end states:

- **Joel** learns the axe's meaning: an ancestral map.
- **John** learns the truth about his people: the guardians.
- **Rafe** chooses between home and the new world.

No ending may leave a thread open. Endings are the run's resolution, not a summary screen — the text should land the specific thing each character now knows or has decided.

## What the artefact must satisfy (the validator is the gate)

`arc.js`'s `validateArc` must accept it with **zero errors**, and the build must be refused if it does not. Specifically, per the engine's rules:

- 3–5 chapters; 2–4 choices per chapter.
- Every choice has a `roll` with a valid `die` and `tier`.
- Every consequence (`success`, `failure`, `complication`, and `bonus` where used) exists, with text, a `next`, and a `resolves` list.
- **`complication.next` must differ from `failure.next`** — a complication is a road, never a retry.
- No dangling `next`, no `self-next`, no cycles, no unreachable chapter or ending.
- Every thread is resolved by every ending.

## How it ships

Authored as an arc source file, validated and bundled by the 2.6/2.7 pipeline's **keyless path** (no API key, no network), written as a plain IIFE script assigning a `window.*` global that `oronath.html` loads with a `<script>` tag. The sample arc keeps working and stays loadable; the real arc becomes the one the surface runs by default.
