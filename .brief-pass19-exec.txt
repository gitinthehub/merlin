NICKEL COAST — ENDING PROSE FIX (pass 19)

Repo: /Users/cloud/Developer/merlin

## Why

An independent review of `arcs/nickel-coast.json` found that all three endings narrate actions the
player may never have taken. Each ending is reachable by **6,152 distinct route chains** (enumerated
from the arc's own consequences), so a sentence that describes an action is false on almost every
route into that ending.

The route-independence rule already applied to Cinder Mile in pass 18: **an ending states what is true
of the world and of all three characters at that ending — never an action the player took, and never a
fact that only one road established.**

The offending sentences, exactly as they stand:

- `end-closed` ("The Season Closes"): "Dez pulls the board and keeps it on the bench", "the table on it
  ends at tonight with nobody's name in front", "Vale signs room 11 out at the bottom of the register,
  one pencil line under forty-one others", "Rook puts his coat on the desk and walks out through the
  office door", and "The breaker goes down" as the first dark ("its first honest dark") — false after a
  route where the breaker already tripped earlier, and after `rook-pay-for-another-week` failure, whose
  own text describes a short stack and a light going out.
- `end-open` ("The Season Never Closes"): "Dez stops fixing the payouts and starts designing them",
  "the high-score table he leaves on the cabinet", "Rook takes the bed in room 11 the way he has taken
  it since June", and "names of people who are standing in the room" — each false on routes where the
  board is on the bench, the player slept on the floor, or nobody was in the room at all.
- `end-tide` ("The Nickel Coast"): "Dez carries a soaked board up the dune with the last line on it
  still legible", "the page for room 11 goes soft in her hands until the four and the one are a smudge
  and the line is closed by weather instead of by hand", and "The coin in his pocket is warm. It stays
  warm for a long time." — the coin is set up only by `rook-pay-for-another-week` failure, whose `next`
  is `end-closed`, and `vale-sign-the-line` success says the quarters stop being warm.

Also: `room-eleven`'s objective is "Decide what room 11 has been keeping." Its three choices are take
the bed, rewire the lamp, or sign the line and close the room — decisions about the room, not about
what it has been keeping. Tighten the objective to name the decision the choices actually offer. This
is a copy fix, not a graph change.

## What to do

Change exactly these fields in `arcs/nickel-coast.json`:

1. The `text` of `end-closed`, `end-open`, `end-tide`.
2. The `objective` of the `room-eleven` chapter.

Then regenerate the bundle: `node tools/build-arc.js --from arcs/nickel-coast.json --out
arcs/nickel-coast.js`. Never hand-edit the bundle.

Nothing else changes: not the ids, not the six chapters, not the nineteen choices, not any choice's
label, roll, or consequence text, not any `resolves` list, not the characters, threads, pitch or blurb.

## What each ending must still do

- **`end-closed` — the season ends properly.** The arcade is shut for the winter, the sign goes off,
  room 11 is signed out, and the three of them leave. Write it as the state of the place and of each
  character at that ending, true whether or not the player pulled the board, rewired the lamp, or
  bought another week.
- **`end-open` — the season never closes.** The sign stays on, the register keeps an open line, room 11
  keeps a guest who pays in quarters. Same rule: state what is true of the place, not what the player
  did.
- **`end-tide` — the coast takes the boardwalk.** The water comes over the pilings, the salt gets into
  the cabinets, and what the coast keeps is the name. Same rule.
- Each ending still names all three characters' fates, because every ending resolves all three threads:
  the table (Dez), the register and room 11 (Vale), the stay (Rook). They are true on every route
  because chapter one puts all three on stage, which is the honest fix the validator's rule demands.
- Keep the three endings distinct from each other. A reader who has seen all three must be able to say
  what differs: the season ended, the season kept running, or the sea ended it.
